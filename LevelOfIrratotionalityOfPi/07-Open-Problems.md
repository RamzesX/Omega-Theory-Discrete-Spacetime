# Module 07 — Open Problems and the Path to Closure

## 7.1 The Remaining Gap

The unconditional dichotomy (Corollary 4.3) separates $e$ and $\pi$ at the level of periodic predictors ($\mathcal{K}_1$). The full separation at all predictor levels ($\mathcal{K}_1$–$\mathcal{K}_5$) requires showing that $\pi$ is Gauss–Kuzmin typical.

We analyzed this assumption via proof by contradiction (assume $\pi \in E$, the exceptional set) and found:

| Case | Description | Status |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Periodic orbit | CF eventually periodic | ✅ **Excluded** (Lindemann + Lagrange) |
| Bounded quotients | All $a_n \leq M$ | ⚠️ Requires $\mu(\pi) > 2$ or $a_n \to \infty$ |
| Positional-polynomial structure | Rule of type $e$ | ⚠️ Empirically excluded (480 tests, 0 fits) |
| Anomalous distribution | Digit frequencies $\neq$ GK | ❌ Open (no known method) |

## 7.2 Statements That Would Close the Proof

The full dichotomy (at all predictor levels) follows from **any one** of:

**(A)** The partial quotients of $\pi$ are unbounded: $\limsup_{n \to \infty} a_n(\pi) = \infty$.

&emsp; *Effect:* Eliminates bounded-quotient case. Combined with empirical exclusion of structured CF, leaves only anomalous distribution.

**(B)** The irrationality measure of $\pi$ satisfies $\mu(\pi) > 2$.

&emsp; *Effect:* Implies unbounded quotients, hence (A). Current best: $\mu(\pi) \leq 7.103$ [Zeilberger–Zudilin, 2020], which is an upper bound. The exact value is unknown.

**(C)** The block complexity of $\mathrm{CF}(\pi)$ satisfies $p(n) > cn^2$ for some $c > 0$.

&emsp; *Effect:* Excludes all positional-polynomial rules (which have $p(n) = O(n^d)$). Currently: $p(n) \geq n + 1$ from Morse–Hedlund.

**(D)** The CF of $\pi$ is not generated by any finite automaton or morphism.

&emsp; *Effect:* Extends Bugeaud's [2013] result from algebraic numbers to $\pi$ specifically.

**(E)** The orbit of $\pi - 3$ under the Gauss map is equidistributed with respect to $\gamma$ (GK-typicality).

&emsp; *Effect:* Full closure — gives the information deficit bound $D(\pi) > 0$ and best-predictor bound $\mathrm{acc} \leq 0.415$.

**Difficulty ranking:** (A) < (B) ≈ (C) < (D) < (E). All are open. All are strictly weaker than normality of $\pi$ in base 10.

## 7.3 The Asymmetric Bet

The conditional theorem has a notable property: **its falsification would be more remarkable than its confirmation.**

If $\pi \in E$ (non-typical), then $\mathrm{CF}(\pi)$ possesses a hidden structure — a positional-polynomial rule, a bounded alphabet, or an anomalous distribution — that has eluded 300 million terms of computation and centuries of mathematical investigation. Such a discovery would:

- Reveal a deep, unexpected connection between the geometry of the circle and modular arithmetic.
- Provide a $O(1)$-per-term algorithm for $\mathrm{CF}(\pi)$, revolutionizing $\pi$ computation.
- Imply $\mu(\pi) = 2$ (if bounded quotients), settling a major open question.

The probability of this outcome, in the informal Bayesian sense, is negligible. But the theorem structure is sound regardless: either the classification holds, or a greater result emerges.
